## B. What the 1852 first-person account establishes

- **30 June:** Isaac (20 years old) left near Council Bluffs with his father, the woman he called mother, brother Abraham and sister Kate; their first campsite was Stringtown. His **biological mother was Emeline Keaton**, deceased in 1844; **Ann Green Carling was the stepmother traveling in 1852** (see independent family roster below). Do NOT reassign biological parentage.
- **5–8 July:** A Missouri River ferry crossing and hard storms preceded organization with Miller's company on 8 July. River crossings and the movement of oxen and wagons occupied much of the first two weeks.
- **12–24 July:** Isaac noted graves seen along the overland route, then the burial of a child belonging to Sarah Galiher/Galliher on **24 July**. The Church History transcript calls Sarah a `sister-in-law`; since Isaac's marriage to **Asenath Browning is recorded in 1854**, this could be retrospective wording or later editorial clarification, not proof of a marriage in 1852.
- **30 July and 23–26 August:** A dance lightened the July 30 camp; further communal dances followed wagon-wheel/tire repair and hunting in late August. The journal describes blacksmithing **as a camp activity** and coal-gathering, but does **not** prove Isaac personally held the occupation of blacksmith.
- **1 August — ANCESTRAL INTERSECTION:** Isaac recorded passing `Curtis' Company` on the Platte. **Uriah Curtis Company** is the named 1852 organization for five-year-old **Edward Milo Webb Jr.** and his mother Caroline Amelia Owens. Isaac's diary dated **1 August**, and [Church History Edward Milo Webb Sr.'s entry](https://history.churchofjesuschrist.org/chd/individual/edward-milo-webb-sr-1815?lang=eng) identifies the elder Webb's death on **31 July 1852** while traveling with Curtis. **Company-level spatial and chronological overlap is a strong identification lead, not evidence that Isaac saw or met the bereaved family, nor that he witnessed Edward Sr.'s death.** This materially connects two previously separate 1852 family journeys without changing an approved person's biography.
- **2 August – early September:** Sand, heavy storms, scarce forage, equipment damage, river crossings and labor continued; by late August he noted both cattle losses and days where dancing followed repair work. A birth is recorded in the company on **17 September** (child and family unnamed in this excerpt).
- **9–10 September:** He passed South Pass on **9 September** and was still traveling west on **10 September**, despite the database's September 10 company end-date field. This is **not** evidence of his own Utah arrival on September 10.
- **29–30 September:** Isaac and companions entered Salt Lake City ahead of the wagon train on **29 September**; the wagons arrived in the city **30 September**, with stormy, cold weather and snow following. [Uriah Curtis Company](https://history.churchofjesuschrist.org/chd/organization/pioneer-company/uriah-curtis-company-1852?lang=eng) separately has its own organization arrival dated **29 September**.

## C. Source conflicts, document handling and precision

1. **Published transcript is not the original:** Text transmitted through the c.1966 Mortenson family history, later rendered online by Church History. Do not claim unaltered manuscript, unique diary page scans, ink, handwriting or original marginalia, nor invent a PDF/image.
2. **Dates:** The Church History person's company window ends **10 September**; Isaac himself continued **to 30 September**. Preserve both with distinct labels. The official transcript jumps from **30 July** to **1 August**; the separately circulating family-history copy includes a **31 July** entry. The second version may represent variant transcription/selection, not a license to silently “correct” the Church History text.
3. **Family:** Isaac's biological mother **Emeline Keaton (died 1844)** was not the adult mother on the 1852 journey. The separately prepared [BYU Winter Quarters / Browning's Camp residents reconstruction](https://winterquartersbyu.earlylds.com/settlements/mills-county/residents-brownings-camp.html) names **John Carling**, his wife **Ann Green**, and Isaac, Catherine/Kate, Abraham, Francis and Joseph as household members; explicitly lists Isaac as Emeline's child. The family-history narrative also says John married Ann after Emeline's death. This is robust compiled-record agreement, not a scanned 1852 family register.
4. **Sarah Galiher burial:** The daughter of the Browning family named Sarah Galiher is called sister-in-law in the published diary even though Isaac's marriage to Asenath Browning occurred later. Treat as later relationship label, possibly retrospective, and verify deceased child's individual record before recording a new person.
5. **Reported wildlife incident:** Several buffalo-tail adventure anecdotes circulate in later Carling family biographies and vary in details. The examined day-by-day Church History diary version reports buffalo hunting **but does not substantiate those elaborate incidents**. Do not present those as verbatim or contemporary journal facts.
6. **Genealogy caution:** The Church History official record of Edward Milo **Webb Jr.** [PER-SYH33H4](https://history.churchofjesuschrist.org/chd/individual/edward-milo-webb-jr-1847?lang=eng) displays an erroneous **1924** death date elsewhere contradicted by the published **1921 civil original** already established in his APPROVED Atlas page. This diary discovery must NOT reopen Webb Jr.'s settled death date or re-review his censuses.

## D. Museum biography material — drafted but NOT published

**Person-centered proposed passage (original synthesis, not a copied diary transcription):**

In the summer of 1852, twenty-year-old Isaac Van Wagoner Carling left the Council Bluffs area with his father John, stepmother Ann Green, and siblings Abraham and Catherine. In his surviving published travel account, the journey appears as a succession of practical problems: getting wagons across rivers, protecting livestock in storms, finding grazing land, repairing tires and procuring meat. The group organized under Henry W. Miller in early July and followed the Platte west. Isaac also noticed people. He recorded graves along the trail, a child's burial, other passing wagon companies and evenings when exhausted travelers danced. The entries show a young man attentive to both the labor that kept his family moving and the small pleasures that made the journey bearable. His route continued through South Pass in September. Though the parent organization has an earlier administrative closing date, Isaac's own account places his arrival in Salt Lake City at the very end of the month.

**Related historical note, separate from the personal biography:** The 1852 wagon migration from the Missouri River to Utah involved independently organized groups that repeatedly passed one another, divided and reassembled along the same route. On 1 August, Isaac recorded passing a company called Curtis's. An official company register places his future son-in-law Edward Milo Webb Jr., then aged five, in Uriah Curtis's train; his father had died the preceding day. There is no record that the families met. The converging timelines show how closely their separate migration stories may have intersected decades before the Carling–Webb marriage connected them.
