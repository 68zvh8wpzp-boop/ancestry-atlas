## 4. Separate material-culture corroboration and cautions

Daughters of Utah Pioneers St. George McQuarrie Memorial Museum publicly lists a **wooden rocker with woven rawhide seat**, museum number **1852** (object/accession number, NOT 1852 construction date), as made in Orderville's United Order period. The museum states the individual craftsman is **unknown** but identifies Isaac Carling as a maker of many such chairs. [Museum exhibit guide, page 24](https://www.dupstgeorge.org/_files/ugd/1d036c_4f7c73a20d4a41fc8b019da10c317e23.pdf). The artifact strengthens the contextual claim that furniture-making was a documented Orderville craft and that Isaac had a reputation for making chairs; **it is NOT proven that Isaac made this specific rocker**. A physical artifact is not a genuine portrait of the maker.

**Later family source (not board minutes):** [Isaac Van Wagoner Carling, family-history biography](https://de.scribd.com/document/48194950/Isaac-Van-Wagoner-Carling) states Isaac was foreman of the carpenter shop, ran the paint shop, produced decorative scrollwork and built the Co-op store with sons **Jesse and Isaac**; it also attributes a bishopric counselor appointment on **5 Aug 1877** and United Order board membership. These claims remain **retrospective family-history statements**, awaiting original MSS 512 evidence. No entry should be invented from a catalog.

## 5. Historical context for his future museum page (distinct from personal life)

The **United Order movement began in 1874** under Brigham Young amid economic insecurity and an ambition to increase religious cooperative self-reliance. **Orderville itself was founded in 1875** as one of the most fully communal experiments. Households pooled labor/production, lived in community housing, ate at a shared dining hall, received community-made clothing and goods, and were overseen by an elected board. Assets grew initially, but tensions over individual expression, property and wages, damage to shared dining facilities, and federal antipolygamy pressure weakened the enterprise. The most fully communal arrangement ended in **1885**, though some cooperatively held enterprises continued later. Sources: [Town of Orderville history](https://orderville.utah.gov/our-history), [Utah History Encyclopedia, Dean May](https://www.preview.uen.org/utah_history_encyclopedia/u/UNITED_ORDER_MOVEMENT.shtml), [Church History Topics, United Orders](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/history/topics/united-orders?lang=eng).

**Atlas structure recommendation:** put that economic/political/social history in **one separate historical context note**, and keep Isaac's eventual museum biography focused on *his individual carpentry, family and board responsibilities only when verified*. Do not turn the whole bio into repeated church history. The broad communal order was **related but distinct** from plural marriage.
