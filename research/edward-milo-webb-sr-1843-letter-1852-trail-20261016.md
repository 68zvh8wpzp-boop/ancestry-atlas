## Identity and vital-life status
Edward Milo Webb Sr., born **17 Aug 1815** Hanover, Chautauqua County, NY, son of James Webb and Hannah Griswold (parentage beyond Webb Sr's generation still subject to separate proof). Wife **Caroline Amelia Owens** (married Dec 1839). Died reportedly **31 July 1852**, age **36**, of cholera during the 1852 overland company journey. Church History Biographical Database profile https://history.churchofjesuschrist.org/chd/individual/edward-milo-webb-sr-1815?lang=eng states "Platte River, Saunders, Nebraska Territory," but this modern geographic labeling is not established by a firsthand death-location record. BYU essay https://rsc.byu.edu/psalms-nauvoo/poet-biographies gives July31, near Platte, relying upon daughter Irene Adell Webb Merrell's 1948 family compilation.

## Material NEW firsthand account: Edward's own published 1843 correspondence
A letter signed **E. M. Webb** and dated **17 April 1843**, published *Times and Seasons*, vol. IV, no. 11, printed pp. **166–167**, is transcribed at https://www.fairlatterdaysaints.org/answers/Times_and_Seasons/4/11 .
Content attributable to Edward's own 1843 writing (first-person primary testimony, but from a modern faithful printed-text transcription rather than agent-inspected original newspaper scan):
- Departed Nauvoo around **3 October 1842**, accompanying H. B. Owens.
- Michigan journey included **Niles**, **Albion**, then **Comstock** about four miles east of Kalamazoo; reported stopping in the Kalamazoo area, returning home afterward and organizing a new local church congregation.
- Describes work and local contacts; avoid padding ancestor personal biography with unnecessary religion. Names include Ezekiel Lee, Lemuel Willard and Jesse Earl but these are associates not identified relatives.
- Carefully distinguish letter date **17 Apr 1843** from issue masthead **15 Apr 1843** (same printed issue), a source date oddity not independently resolved.
This is higher-value direct-person life evidence than previous compiled migration summary. Check page original image before claiming an **original image capture**; no new media asset downloaded here.

## 1852 contemporary trail diary: independent Curtis-company proximity
The complete edited transcription of the **Isaac Van Wagoner Carling** 1852 diary, housed at the Church History Biographical Database:
https://history.churchofjesuschrist.org/chd/transcript?name=transcript-for-carling-isaac-v-diary-in-elda-mortenson-comp-isaac-v-carling
- **23 July 1852:** Carling's company was on the road after Fort Kearny, on/near the Platte route.
- **30 July:** record mentions Carrion Creek camp.
- **1 August:** Carling wrote that his company passed **"Curtis' Company"** and continued toward sand hills westward along the Platte. This is contemporary, **independent evidence that Curtis's caravan was at a location west of Fort Kearny by Aug 1**, subject to the edited-transcript's route description.
- It does **not** say Edward Webb was in that precise place on July 31, does **not** record his death, and does **not** identify the site of burial. It is an **adjacent-day** geographic context lead, not independent confirmation of his death.

## Crucial burial-geopinning caution
- The Church History database names **Saunders County, Nebraska Territory** as death place. **Nebraska Territory did not exist in 1852** (established 1854), and **Saunders County was created in 1856**; location labels are obviously retrospective.
- Saunders County is **eastern** Nebraska, whereas Fort Kearny is in **south-central** Nebraska (NPS Fort Kearny site: https://home.nps.gov/places/000/fort-kearny-state-historical-park.htm ). The August 1 companion-company diary suggests Curtis was **west of Fort Kearny**, making the Saunders County assignment questionable for the July 31 stop. **Do not assert that death definitely occurred farther west** without Edward's own company day-by-day log; company movements need careful reconciliation.
- For Atlas born/lived/died pin rules: leave **“near the Platte River on the 1852 overland trail”** as approximate geographic description with **no fabricated county, exact grave/pin, or historical Nebraska Territory**. If a pin is demanded later, represent provisional corridor/uncertain location in data, not a false exact point.
