## Source-critical results

### A. First marriage: Asenath Browning

**Supported family:** Isaac and Asenath are widely recorded as husband and wife, Sarah their eldest daughter; Isaac was 22 and Asenath 18 at reported **17 November 1854** wedding. Sarah's birth is reported **25 February 1856**. The BYU sketch additionally places married residence at Ogden before a later move to Fillmore. This family structure is strong as a working conclusion, not individually primary-original-certified here.

**Material contradiction NEWLY IDENTIFIED and adjudicated:**
- FamilySearch's 1854 marriage place is **Ogden** (a standardized assembled record).
- BYU sketch and another compiled family story place the marriage/sealing in **Salt Lake City Endowment House**.
- Institutionally verified building fact: **that building did not exist until April 1855**; therefore the claim of an **1854 wedding inside that specific Endowment House building is chronologically impossible**.
- The broader possibility of a wedding in 1854 elsewhere, followed by an ordinance after May 1855, is *plausible but NOT demonstrated by an original*. The Council House upstairs was also no longer the routinely used sealing site by November 1854 according to official history. **Do not silently substitute either building, or choose Ogden/Salt Lake with false certainty.**
- Recommended current Atlas phrasing pending original: `Isaac married Asenath Elizabeth Browning, reportedly 17 November 1854 in Utah Territory; contemporary ceremony location and distinction from any later sealing remain unverified. Their first daughter Sarah Elizabeth was born at Ogden in 1856 according to a later biographical sketch.`
- A firsthand dated entry from church/county marriage or ordinance register is necessary to establish exact location and distinguish civil marriage, living sealing and later proxy ordinances. **No such image/transcription was obtained.**

### B. Second marriage: Miriam Elizabeth Hobson

**Reported:** 27 August 1857, Salt Lake City (FamilySearch, compiled Frank pedigree and family-history story), with at least four children in published family reconstructions; Miriam died in 1869. Asenath remained alive until 1899. **The concurrent life spans and separately named children strongly support a two-wife plural-family reconstruction**; original marriage/household/sealing record should still be checked before promoting the *specific marriage date, venue and exact offspring assignment* to fully original-proven.

**Chronology warning:** Miriam's birth is reported as **31 August 1843**; an 27 August 1857 wedding would have been **four days before her fourteenth birthday** (age 13 years, 11 months, 27 days). Both dates currently come from **compiled/pedigree material**, so this should be a specific original-document verification priority rather than a conclusively asserted child marriage. Do not flatten a reported sealing date into a legal marital ceremony without checking the original column meanings. Posthumous twentieth-century ordinance records are not proof of marriages during Isaac's lifetime.

### C. Sarah Elizabeth's direct parentage

BYU's named Sarah biography explicitly links her to **Isaac (also styled Van Wagner) and Asenath**, born **25 February 1856**, Ogden. The previously audited 1860 Fillmore household also carries an indexed name variant `Carlen` and young Sarah with the parents. **Do not redo the 1860 census**: already inspected in prior Sarah audit. This tranche contributes distinct published narrative corroboration and disentangles mother Asenath from Miriam; it **does not** add an original 1856 civil birth certificate. [Utah Archives birth-record guide](https://archives.utah.gov/research/guides/birth/) states Utah did not require governmental birth registration before **1898**; therefore no routine statewide 1856 birth certificate should be expected. Church ward blessing/member registers may be more appropriate original types.
