## Source A: compiled Carling family-history narrative — reported 4 August 1876 departure

**Citation and access:** `Isaac Van Wagoner Carling`, compiled family biography, online 48-page reproduction at https://de.scribd.com/document/48194950/Isaac-Van-Wagoner-Carling, section immediately preceding heading `The United Order`, current web-readable text around lines **653–670**. This is a later family-history compilation / recollection, **NOT Isaac's handwritten 1876 diary**, an original emigrant roster or signed 1876 travel list.

**Actual reported event:** `August 4, 1876` **departure / journey south from Fillmore**, destination Orderville. Not explicitly an 4 August **arrival**. Text lists **three covered wagons** and the journeying family, woodworking lathe, scroll saw/carpenter tools, violin/melodeon, plants/seeds and other belongings. Provides relative ages:
- Miriam Hobson's four children as John Henry **15**, Ellen Elvira **12**, Lydia May **10**, Jesse Hobson **7**;
- Asenath's living children as Sarah Elizabeth **20**, Asenath Emeline **18**, Ann **17**, Laura **16**, Catherine Aurelia **11**, Martha Jane **9**, Phoebe Malinda **6**, Isaac Jr **3**, Mary Alice **2**, Miriam Eliza **1**. 
- The narrative explicitly says **Olive had died before the move** and **Barbara Amelia was born later**, so the working migration roster is *ten Asenath + four Miriam children* (**14** living traveling children by its enumeration), not 16 physically present. This is a count of **reported names**, not a new first-hand household certificate.

**Chronological assessment:** The presented daughter Sarah's reported 25 February 1856 birth yields age **20 on 4 August 1876** but **19 on 11 August 1875**. Other enumerated children's stated ages are likewise broadly consistent with 1876 (exact individual birth dates were **not re-verified** in this tranche). Narrative's explicit age 20 for Sarah provides a concrete internal reason to *prefer 1876* **if** their recorded ages and date were copied accurately; ages may have been retrospectively calculated to fit the date and therefore are not independent original corroboration.

## Source B: 1965 Chamberlain family reunion compilation — reported 11 August 1875 arrival

**Citation and access:** `Thomas Chamberlain Jr. Family Reunion History July 1965`, FamilySearch **Memory 2501673**: https://www.familysearch.org/en/memories/memory/2501673, section `ELLEN ALVIRA CARLING CHAMBERLAIN`, page-like marker **15** in publicly indexed transcription. The narrative explicitly introduces the remembered migration account: `Of Ellen and her family, her daughter, Annie, wrote the following:` followed by a date reported as **11 August 1875**, saying Isaac's household arrived at Orderville to join the United Order. It reports three wagons, a lathe, melodeon, other instruments, and `thirteen girls and three boys`. FamilySearch's public indexed text is accessible although authenticated underlying page images are **not captured or independently collated**.

**Source weight:** This is a **twentieth-century account attributed to a descendant writing about her grandmother's family history**, printed in a 1965 reunion collection. It is **not Ellen's own 1875 diary** or an original Orderville member-register entry. The reported **16 traveling children** is difficult to reconcile with the other family-history enumeration of fourteen living named travelers, one Asenath daughter already deceased, and another not yet born; do not infer 16 distinct living children in one wagon migration simply because the memoir says so.

**Relationship to source A:** Both mention **three covered wagons**, **Isaac's lathe**, and **household music**. The shared distinctive details suggest overlapping family tradition or narrative dependence; **they should NOT be treated as two independent contemporary corroborations**. An 1875 arrival and an August 1876 initial departure from Fillmore are **incompatible for a single direct move**, absent unproven back-and-forth travel. Do not invent a repeated trip as a reconciliation.
