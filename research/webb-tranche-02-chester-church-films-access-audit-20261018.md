## 2. Independent checks: precisely which alternative is worth trying

Connecticut State Library church research guide: https://libguides.ctstatelibrary.org/hg/church/home confirms hundreds of **digitized church microfilms** are browsable by *Place* search from FamilySearch, while the separate [2024 newly digitized churches list](https://libguides.ctstatelibrary.org/hg/church/fam) does **not** name Chester in its highlighted new projects. That omission has no bearing on previously microfilmed Chester copies, so do **not** infer nondigitization.

Connecticut State Library's official [Church Records Index guide](https://libguides.ctstatelibrary.org/c.php?g=211782&p=11144255) states its pre-1850 church event index covers **roughly one-quarter** of State Library church holdings. Where an index slip exists, it generally includes event type and original *town/church/volume/page* citation. Thus **one named Chester Third Church index slip for James Webb/Elizabeth Douglas or a c.1777 James child** would be materially more valuable than another copy of Bailey's 1896 typed marriage list; **absence of a slip would not disprove the event**.

The established 1896 printed derivative marriage source `Frederic W. Bailey, Early Connecticut Marriages, Second Book, pp.89–90` already shows a James Webb–Elizabeth Douglas marriage in 1773 under Chester Third Church with no groom's age, father, occupation or child list. **The actual persons involved remain unresolved**. Modern FamilySearch's **James Webb born 1754 (LHSH-TKQ)** candidate identifies Elizabeth Douglas and children including **Stephen born 1777**, NOT the specific James Jr later in Nauvoo; that modern tree cannot prove a marriage identity. The **James born 1733 (LKKM-KR5)** candidate separately ties to an Elizabeth Welch/Nova Scotia family line. Keep different men separated until a parent-child original or collateral probate chain actually identifies the 1777 ancestor.
