## 1. Independent reinspection of Bailey's scanned 1896 source

Frederic W. Bailey (editor), *Early Connecticut Marriages as Found on Ancient Church Records Prior to 1800*, Second Book (New Haven, 1896), **printed pages 89–90** (the page 90 marriage line). Digitized full book:
https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/cc/Early_Connecticut_marriages_as_found_on_ancient_church_records_prior_to_1800_%28IA_cu31924092205818%29.pdf

At printed page **89**, beginning of the church section, Bailey explains **Third Church, founded 1742 in the old town of Saybrook, now Chester**. He writes that most of this church's earlier records were lost, and that only a limited set of marriages was preserved. On printed **page 90**, the marriage appears within **this SAME Third Church/Chester section**, as `James Webb & Elizabeth Douglas, Dec. 9` under the 1773 grouping. The neighboring list includes other Webb marriages, but NO ages, parent-child relationship, birthplace, or occupation identifying the groom. It is an **1896 printed derivative transcription**, NOT the 1773 autograph manuscript and not evidence that any of the original 1773 leaves still exist.

Separate textual verification: *Third Congregational Church, Saybrook Connecticut Early Marriage Records*:
https://www.newhorizonsgenealogicalservices.com/connecticut-genealogy/middlesex/saybrook_ct_marriage_records_1800.htm
This explicitly heads the same 1773 marriage under the **Third Congregational Church / Chester** and says surviving marriage entries are a remnant of missing church records. It is **a secondary reprint of Bailey**, not independent proof of a parent-child relationship.
