EDWARD MILO WEBB JR.

Edward Milo Webb Jr. son of Edward Milo Webb Sr. and Caroline Amelia Owens was
born 8 March 1847, Linden, Atchison Co., Missouri. He died 11 September 1921 at
Murray, Utah and buried at Holladay, Utah. He Married Ellen Ashman on the 6th of
June 1870, at Salt Lake City, Utah. He later married (2) Sara Elizabeth Carling;
(3) Charlotte Maxwell.

Edward Milo Jr. when but five years of age, walked most of the way across the
plains and drove a small herd of sheep for his widowed mother. She located in the
new settlement of Fillmore upon reaching the Valley.

Although unable to attend a school, (he began work at seven years of age) he
was taught the rudiments of learning by his mother. Edward Milo alert and eager for
knowledge, through diligently applying himself, became especially skilled in math-
ematics, linguists and other subjects and fitted himself for a teacher of exceptional
ability. He was a self taught man, though, one winter he had training under C.H.
Riggs and John R. Pack in the University of Dereret. While yet in his teens, he
taught evening classes for men, some of them being twice his age, he then taught
in a district school and later became County Superintendent of Millard Schools in
Utah, first principal of the Snowflake Academy in Arizona and recommended by Dr. Karl
G. Maeser, through Apostle Teasdale, the principalship of the Juarez Academy in Mexico.
For the sake of his health, however, which years of sedentary life had ruined, he had
to give up the profession for which he was so well adapted and do out-of-doors work as
government surveying, running brick yards, and farming.

Edward Milo was prominent in church as well as civil affairs and, as a pioneer,
he knew first hand all the struggles and hardships incident to such a life. Moving
to Fillmore in its early days, he grew up along with the town and as an official of
the order and councilor in the bishopric, he helped to establish the town of Order-
ville in Utah. He was the second bishop in Woodruff, Arizona, and helped settle Morelos
and other towns in Mexico.

At sixty-five years of age when broken in health and at an age when life should
have been easier, he, with all the other colonists in Mexico during the Madero Revolu-
tion of 1912, was forced to leave everything and flee to the United States, there to
begin anew to make a home. By this time he had had a large and splended family who,
after a few years were all married and located in Arizona, Utah and a few other states.

The children, after their father's death, have kept in touch with each other by
meeting occasionally, in family reunions and by circulating mimeographed family news
bulletins. Eight of these bulletins have been bound together to make a book covering
the years from 1938 to 1943. A more detailed account of his life is given in the book
EDWARD MILO WEBB - HIS ANCESTORS AND DESCENDENTS and it gives, also, a life sketch of
his ancestors and the genealogy of his descendents.
