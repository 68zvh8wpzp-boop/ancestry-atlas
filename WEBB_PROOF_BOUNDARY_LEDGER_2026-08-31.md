# Webb proof-boundary ledger — 31 August 2026

## Current public boundary

The supported Webb trunk remains:

Jeremy Webb → James Sheldon Webb → James Wilford “Jay” Webb → Jonathan Henry
Webb → Edward Milo Webb Jr. → Edward Milo Webb Sr. → James Webb Jr.

The relationship from James Webb Jr. to the reported James Webb and Elizabeth
Douglas remains a research-frontier claim. Gideon Webb, Edith Bates and the
proposed older New England and English lines remain provisional or frontier only.

## What changed in this audit

### Located: the candidate couple's marriage

Frederic W. Bailey's 1896 published transcript of surviving Third Church of
Saybrook records lists **James Webb and Elizabeth Douglas, married 9 December
1773**. The church became the Congregational church at Chester. Bailey states
that most of the old church's records had been lost.

This is materially better evidence than a repeated online tree assertion. It
establishes that a couple with the reported names married in the relevant
Middlesex County locality before James Webb Jr.'s reported 21 March 1777 birth.
It does **not** identify their children or prove that James Jr. was their son.

Source: [Bailey, *Early Connecticut Marriages*, Saybrook/Chester entries](https://archive.org/details/cu31924092205818).

### Located: a compatible 1790 locality cluster

The published 1790 federal census schedules place a **James Webb** household in
Saybrook town. The same town list contains several Webb households and a Daniel
Douglass household. This makes Saybrook/Chester a higher-priority cluster for
record retrieval, but the head-of-household list supplies no relationships and
does not identify the 1773 groom conclusively.

Source: [U.S. Census Office, *Heads of Families … 1790: Connecticut*](https://archive.org/details/headsoffamiliesa06unit).

### Located: a probate retrieval target

The Connecticut State Library's official directory indicates a **James Webb**
probate packet in the Saybrook Probate District's Webb range. Chester probate
matters were handled by that district. The packet has not yet been examined, so
its decedent, date, heirs and contents are unknown.

Source: [Connecticut State Library Probate Estate Files Directory](https://libguides.ctstatelibrary.org/ProbateFilesDirectory).

## Ranked retrieval queue

1. **Saybrook probate packet for James Webb.** Obtain every image, including
   cover, bond, inventory, distribution, receipts and loose papers. Abstract all
   heirs, administrators, witnesses, creditors, land descriptions and dates.
2. **1790 Saybrook census image.** Capture the original page and household
   columns for James Webb; record adjacent names and compare them with the 1773
   marriage list and later deeds/probate.
3. **Chester/Saybrook land and tax records, 1773–1825.** Build a chronology for
   every adult James Webb and every Douglas/Douglass household; extract grantor,
   grantee, spouse releases, witnesses and abutters.
4. **Church and town records.** Search surviving Chester/Saybrook material plus
   Chatham/East Hampton, East Haddam and neighboring congregations for baptisms,
   admissions, dismissals, marriages and deaths.
5. **Western New York bridge.** Trace James Jr. and Hannah Griswold backward from
   the 1815 Hanover birth of Edward Milo Webb using Chautauqua County deeds,
   tax lists, censuses, church records and associate households. A Connecticut
   origin named in a New York record could solve the problem from the destination
   side.
6. **Only after parentage is resolved:** test Gideon Webb and Edith Bates, then
   the proposed seventeenth-century New England line. Attempt England last.

## Stop rules

- Do not promote the 1773 couple to parents because the names, date and locality
  are persuasive.
- Do not choose between 1733–1817 and 1754–1823 without record-level identity
  evidence.
- Do not merge Elizabeth Douglas profiles until spouses, residences, relatives
  and dates resolve to one person.
- Do not use surname resemblance, copied pedigrees or flag imagery to cross the
  Atlantic.
- Preserve James Webb Jr. as the visible proof boundary until the parentage claim
  passes the evidence-promotion contract.

## Product implication

This case is the model for scalable research: the system must distinguish a
proved marriage from an unproved parent-child edge, retain competing identities,
queue the underlying record behind every index hit, and let a reviewer approve a
new source without silently promoting the family tree.
