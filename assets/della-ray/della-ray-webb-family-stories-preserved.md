## My Grandma by Kayle McNeil

Contributed Cherryl McNeil Beal, 19 June 2017; upload `story.txt`. Author identified in title as Kayle McNeil.

She was my grandma, and mine alone, or at least that is how she made each of us feel. I was special to her, and I'm sure she made each one of all the grandchildren feel that way (I really do feel that I was her favorite). I remember her living in the green house by Rainbow Lake. She was short and cuddly. She wore white gloves, smelled of powder, wore earrings, and was the prettiest grandmother in the world. I never saw or heard her get angry. I never heard her talk bad about anyone. She was the center of the family; at least in my eyes. I remember the smell of fresh baked bread.

I and my son Jonathan have her earlobes. I am proud of them, and even though Jonathan never knew her, I tell him about her, and his heritage of having her earlobes. he wears them proudly too.

She was full of love and charity. She never thought of herself. I miss her terribly. Sometimes I know I made her feel bad when I was rotten. I remember way back when I was real little. We kids would get to take turns sleeping with her. I remember her climbing into bed with a big sigh. She would always say out loud to Heavenly Father, "Thanks for the bed."

She never complained about any of her illnesses. The weekend before she died, Vicki and I came up to show her our latest addition to the family, Jason. That Saturday, the whole family met in the church cultural hall. I can't remember if we ate there or not. I do know all the kids were roller-skating on the wooden floor. I was pushing grandma around in her wheel chair. As I look back, she was acting a little different, in that she didn't talk much or wasn't acting quite herself. I saw in her eyes that she was enjoying all the grandchildren, however. I feel now that she knew she was about to die, and she was taking in all the family one last time. Had I known she was going to die that week I would never have left her. I just never thought grandma could die. I am so thankful that I did get to see her one last time. I Love You Grandma!

PS - There was one time grandma was cooking something in the oven, and she stuck her head in the oven to check the pilot light. It ignited and singed her eyebrows and hair. Another time Raymond, PeeWee, and I stuck her on the back of a motorcycle at a family reunion and drove her around. Grandma was plucky and neat. She was game for anything. She just loved family!

## Tribute to Grandma from the Grandchildren by Raymond D. Webb

Contributed Cherryl McNeil Beal, 19 June 2017; upload `story.txt`. Author identified in title as Raymond D. Webb. Funeral program independently lists “Tribute to Grandma: Raymond Webb”; the uploaded text has no explicit composition date, so identity as the precise delivered script is not assumed.

It is my honor to express a few memories of the grandchildren concerning the character and virtues of Grandma. My first encounter with Grandma was an impressive one that changed my whole way of life. The doctor was unable to make the journey to Vernon in time and Grandma brought me into this life. Although my first experience is unique, it is typical of the way Grammy influenced each Grandchild.

It was so easy to love Grandma because her biggest concern was the welfare of her family, both spiritual and mental as well as physical. always concerned about the inter-relationships of the family, she never missed a chance to teach the gospel and character-building attributes interwoven with everyday living.

Words were never a stranger to Grandma. she was never afraid to express her feelings. One day while living in Mesa behind Aunt Louise's, Jean and I drove to visit her. When we walked in Daddy was visiting her too. I walked over and hugged and kissed her then just shook hands with my father. Grandma said, "You don't be afraid to hug your father, and even kiss him!" So, I did, mainly for her benefit, and she radiated with joy as she continued to visit with us.

With her legs failing her, it gave the grandchildren many opportunities to do for her and check on her. Karen had many such opportunities and as a result visited a lot with Grandma. Knowing how Grandma awaited the birth of each new great grandchild, Karen had Grandma promise she would live long enough to see her first baby. Grandma passes away three days after fulfilling that promise. Many such memories are ours to share as grandchildren of Della Webb.

## My Dear, Sweet Grandmother - Della Ray Webb

Contributed Cherryl McNeil Beal, 26 March 2017; upload `story.txt`. First-person grandchild account; no separate author byline displayed.

Who didn't love going to Grandma Webb's home. To me it was the GREATEST and GRANDEST place on earth! I remember vividly, the long drive along a winding road through Salt River Canyon, for the Valley to Grandpa and Grandma's home in Lakeside. I would fall asleep until we hit the pine trees, the magnificent fragrance waking me up. Mom told me I would sit up and say, "My mountains, my mountains. That smell held fond memories for me.

Oh my Angel Grandmother, how I loved you, I love your smell, fresh and powdery. On special days when you were going to Church or somewhere else special, you smelled like tea roses, a cream sold by Avon, I believe.

Grandma had an aluminum cup with a small embossed flower on it, I think it came out of an oatmeal box. The icy cold water tasted SO delicious out of that cup, and I think everyone in Lakeside had at one time or another, drank out of it.

I remember coming up in the wintertime, there was no heat in the back bedrooms when I was younger; Grandma would let me sleep with her and put my cold feet on her belly to get warm. As an arthritis sufferer, she would rub witch hazel on her sore joints.

Grandma was an excellent seamstress, and made me some shirts out of the flowered material off of flour sacks. She was always cooking, or crocheting, or sewing. When she made bread, I would always sneak pinches of the dough, it smelled so good and yeasty. I loved the smells in her kitchen. When the bread was done, what could be tastier than a warm slice with butter melted into every little hole. She had a pantry that ALWAYS had pies, or cakes, cookies, or something else to tantalize the taste buds- not to mention her famous, sticky cinnamon buns! When she made pies, she would put cinnamon and sugar on them, bake them, and give them to the Grand-kids. Grandma wore an apron, and was never without one when she was in her kitchen.

Every morning she would wind open the kitchen window that looked out over Rainbow Lake, she loved the fresh air. We would hear the blue jays, scolding each other in the trees. There were sparrows, cardinals, magpies, and orioles, seeking refuge and bits of food. It wasn't uncommon to find Grandma sitting outside in her metal lawn chair, watering her lawn or flowers, enjoying the fresh air and waving at neighbors and friends as they drove by. I don't know a soul who didn't love her, or know her.

I used to chuckle to myself when she would say, "fiddle faddle", and how she shouldn't "cuss like that". Or when she would look for her glasses and ask me to help her find where she left them, as I would giggle and tell her, "Grandma, they're on your head!"

When our Family wasn't quite so large, we would have Thanksgiving at Grandma's house. What a thrill it was to listen to 3 of her 4 daughters, working merrily in the kitchen, preparing a sumptuous feast. I have NO clue where the men were or what they were doing. Grand-kids were everywhere, being Grand-kids!

There was bottling to do, and I got to help shell the peas, snap the beans, peel and slice the peaches. The first cook stove she had was a wood burning stove, she heated water on it, cook on it, and heated with it. Before indoor plumbing, she would heat water and pour it into a #2 tub, for baths.

I loved everything there was to love about this dear, sweet, patient Grandma of mine. I loved her more than words can express!

## Correlation and limits

- Raymond's account names Vernon as the place where Della delivered him before the doctor's arrival. Della's own handwritten life sketch describes delivering her first grandson. These compatible accounts strengthen the identification; no professional qualification inferred.
- Kayle's final-weekend recollection and Raymond's great-grandchild account are personal recollections. Neither proves Della's private knowledge that death was imminent.
- Mesa winter residence is supported by the reunion report and Raymond's memory of visits behind Louise's. It is compatible with Lakeside as usual residence; it contradicts an assumption of uninterrupted year-round Lakeside residence.
- Green house/Rainbow Lake, flour-sack sewing, bottling produce and wood-stove/tub memories offer concrete household details. Dates of stove or plumbing changes are unspecified.
- The reunion's six-child Jay/Marion entry agrees with the approved six-child account and disagrees with truncated lists in later family histories. The deceased son is unnamed here.
- Douglas's death and Kim's burns in the 26 September 1960 house fire are reported family events; contemporary fire/death/newspaper records remain to be correlated.
