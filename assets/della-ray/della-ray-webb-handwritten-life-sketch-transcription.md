## Reading and evidence notes

- Page 1's handwritten colony name looks like “oxaco”; Oaxaca is the corresponding place in the compiled family history, but that normalized spelling is not substituted in the original block.
- Some overwritten and crossed-out words cannot be recovered securely. Their uncertainty is preserved rather than guessed. The principal surviving narrative is legible throughout.
- The “My family of more or less been” phrase is retained as written; it is not silently rewritten into fluent prose.
- Departure for the United States is explicitly dated 12 August 1912. The manuscript does not explicitly date the Douglas border arrival to that same day.
- The manuscript attributes the upheaval and destruction to Pancho Villa. This remains retrospective testimony requiring historical correlation; it is not proof of an identified raid.
- The writer names her father James Wilford Ray, contrary to the Webb surname typed in her death certificate. Neither original is silently corrected.
- Grandchild counts describe an undated point in her lifetime. They are not final descendant totals.
- Three deliveries before a doctor's arrival support her own account of practical care, not an inferred licensed profession.
