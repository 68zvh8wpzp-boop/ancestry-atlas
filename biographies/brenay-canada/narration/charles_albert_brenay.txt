Charles Albert Brenay was born on March 29, 1897, in Arnstein, Ontario, the son of Charles Godfrey Brenay and Ida Mae Gooley. Arnstein lay among the forest settlements of the Parry Sound District, connected to Georgian Bay and the Great Lakes through timber, railways and rough roads. His birth placed the family’s Canadian origin in a specific northern Ontario community.

As a boy, Charles crossed into Michigan with his parents and grew up in Alpena. The Lake Huron city was shaped by lumber mills, docks and rail lines. By 1910, the Brenay household was established there, part of a borderland in which work and family ties routinely joined Ontario and Michigan.

In June 1918, during the final year of the First World War, he registered for the American draft in Alpena under the name Albert Brenay. On that public record he identified Canada as the country whose citizen or subject he remained. The card is one of the clearest surviving documents connecting his adult identity to his Canadian birth.

Charles married Marian Beulah Skinner, and their family began in Michigan. Their daughter Marion was born in Saginaw in 1923. In 1925, Charles moved the young household west to Arizona, where he built homes, repaired machinery, operated shoe shops and pursued land and practical work.

Charles lived until 1983. His life joined an Ontario birth, a Great Lakes upbringing and an American Southwest future. Through him, the Brenay line carried a documented Canadian beginning across the border and into the later Webb family.
