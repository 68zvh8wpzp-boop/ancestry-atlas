Edward Milo Webb Sr. was born on August 17, 1815, in Hanover, Chautauqua County, New York. He was the son of James Webb Jr. and Hannah Griswold. Western New York was then a young settlement region, and the Erie Canal—completed when Edward was ten—was beginning to transform movement, trade and migration across the state.

Edward married Caroline Amelia Owens on December 21, 1839. Caroline had been born in Warren, Ohio, in 1821. Their early married years carried them through the restless world of the 1840s, when families, faith communities and commercial networks moved repeatedly through New York, Ohio, Illinois and Missouri.

Historical records place Edward in Nauvoo, Illinois, and in travel through the surrounding region. Contemporary journals and an autobiography later written by his son preserve the outline of those unsettled years as the family moved through communities on the western edge of American settlement.

The couple’s children included Edward Milo Webb Jr., born in Missouri in 1847. By then the family had already experienced several moves. Their route belonged to a much larger nineteenth-century migration through the old Northwest and the Missouri frontier, where new towns rose quickly and households often relocated in search of community, land and security.

In June 1852, Edward, Caroline and their children joined the Uriah Curtis Company to cross the plains. The company list preserves the family as a traveling group: Edward and Caroline, their children Cordelia, Marcellus, Edward Jr. and Caroline, together with Mary Jane Webb and James Hofer. Their journey began as part of an organized migration, not as an isolated wagon moving alone.

Edward did not complete the crossing. He died on July 31, 1852, near the Platte River in Nebraska Territory, only thirty-six years old. Later accounts describe the broader disease and hardship of the trail, but no cause of death has been established for Edward himself.

Caroline continued west with the children. The company reached the Salt Lake Valley in September, and five-year-old Edward Jr. survived to carry the family line into Utah and later Arizona. Edward Sr.’s short life therefore became a hinge in the Webb migration: from western New York through the Midwest to the Platte River, with the route completed by the family he left behind.

James Webb Jr. and Hannah Griswold are supported as Edward’s parents. Beyond James Jr., however, the evidence changes character. Conflicting identities and provisional connections mark the present boundary between the supported Webb story and the generations that remain under investigation.
