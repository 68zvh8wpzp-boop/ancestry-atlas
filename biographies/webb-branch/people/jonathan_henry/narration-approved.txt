Jonathan Henry Webb was born on June 21, 1886, in Woodruff, Arizona Territory, the son of Edward Milo Webb Jr. and Sarah Elizabeth Carling. Woodruff stood near the Little Colorado River in a landscape where irrigation, livestock and close cooperation could make the difference between a settlement that endured and one that disappeared.

Arizona was still twenty-six years from statehood. Railroads were linking the territory to national markets, but daily life in the northeastern settlements remained shaped by water, weather and distance. Jonathan’s father belonged to an older generation that had crossed the plains and then moved south into Arizona. His mother, Sarah, managed a large household in that unsettled borderland.

Jonathan’s own life soon crossed the international boundary. On August 16, 1908, he married Della Ray in Colonia Morelos, Sonora. Della had been born in Colonia Pacheco in 1891, the daughter of James Wilford Ray and Elsie Margaret Mortensen. Their marriage joined the Webb line to a family whose route reached through Utah and Denmark into the agricultural colonies of northern Mexico.

The couple’s first children were born in Colonia Morelos. Two years after their marriage, the Mexican Revolution began, bringing violence, political upheaval and repeated displacement to northern Mexico. The surviving family compilation establishes the family’s Mexican beginnings and later Arizona household, but the exact date and immediate cause of Jonathan and Della’s move north remain unresolved.

By 1924, when their son James Wilford—Jay—was born, the family was in Pinedale, Arizona. Pinedale belonged to the forested White Mountains rather than the desert Arizona of postcards. Timber, livestock, small farms and seasonal employment shaped the community. Jonathan and Della raised a large family whose children and grandchildren spread through places such as Pinedale, Lakeside, Vernon, Snowflake and McNary.

Their household lived through Arizona statehood, two world wars and the Great Depression. Roads improved, automobiles shortened journeys and the timber economy drew families among mountain towns, but isolation did not vanish quickly. The Webb family’s geography remained a network of relatives, mills, farms and small communities across northeastern Arizona.

Della died in McNary in 1969. Jonathan lived until 1975. Their marriage formed the bridge between a territorial Arizona childhood, the family’s years in northern Mexico and the White Mountains world in which Jay and later James Sheldon were raised. The broad route is supported, while civil, census and border records may eventually supply more exact dates and reasons for individual moves.
