Edward Milo Webb Jr. was born on March 8, 1847, at Linden in northwestern Missouri. He was the son of Edward Milo Webb Sr. and Caroline Amelia Owens. His birth came as the United States was at war with Mexico and as families continued moving through the Mississippi and Missouri borderlands toward an uncertain West.

In 1852, when Edward was five, the family joined the Uriah Curtis Company for the journey across the plains. The company record places his parents, siblings and other members of their traveling group together. On July 31, beside the Platte River in Nebraska Territory, his father died. Caroline continued west with the children, reaching the Salt Lake Valley in September.

Family recollection says that Edward helped move livestock during the crossing. What is certain is the larger fact: he crossed the plains as a small child and arrived without his father.

Edward grew to adulthood in Utah Territory during years of rapid settlement and conflict. A modern historical account places him at Cove Fort in 1867 and connects him with the territorial Black Hawk War. Those episodes belong to the documented outline of his life, while the precise details still rest mainly on family and institutional histories rather than a complete personal archive.

As an adult, Edward moved into Arizona Territory. By 1890, an original surviving letter records him acting with Jesse Nathaniel Smith. Other historical sources describe him as an educator and community leader in the Snowflake area. His life had moved from a Missouri birthplace and an overland childhood to the institution-building years of territorial Arizona.

Edward’s family was large and complex. His marriage to Sarah Elizabeth Carling carried the Webb line through their son Jonathan Henry Webb, born at Woodruff in 1886. A family photograph taken in 1891 identifies Sarah with five of her children: Clark, Jonathan Henry—listed as Henry—Burr, Owen and Catherine.

Edward’s death date carries a surviving conflict. A Utah death-certificate index, the published Webb family account and several derivative records converge on September 11, 1921, in Murray, Utah. One structured institutional profile instead reports April 4, 1924, although that same profile’s portrait caption says 1847 to 1921. Taken together, the evidence strongly supports 1921, while the 1924 date remains an anomaly and the direct certificate image remains an important research goal.

Edward Milo Webb Jr.’s supported story is substantial: a child carried into the western migration, a father lost beside the Platte, an adult life in Utah and Arizona, and a descendant line rooted in the White Mountains. Additional records may sharpen individual episodes, but the broad course of his life is already well supported.
