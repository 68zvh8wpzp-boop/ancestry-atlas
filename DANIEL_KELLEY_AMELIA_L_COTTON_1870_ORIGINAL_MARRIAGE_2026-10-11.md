## Genealogical evaluation

1. **Amelia Lucinda Cotton's identity materially strengthened.** The original 1870 marriage supplies bride **Amelia L. Cotton, 17, born New York** and witness **Betsey A. Cotton**. Those details agree with the reported 1853 birth, mother Betsey Ann Brockway Cotton and 1886 Montague family book.
2. **Three marital surnames documented through three original returns:** `1870: Amelia L Cotton marries Daniel Kelley` → `1879: Amelia [surname/initial handwritten ambiguous, indexed Cotton] marries Jonas Aurin` → `1888: Amelia Aurin, maiden surname Amelia Cotton, marries Smith McConnell`. **1888 original specifically reports two prior marriages**, consistent with the 1870 and 1879 originals. The fact they are three separate civil marriage ceremonies does NOT establish dissolution mechanism. No divorce or death record for Daniel or Jonas has been verified.
3. **Older girls in Jonas's 1880 household**: Iva (5) and Frances (3) were born approximately 1875 and 1877, during Amelia's documented 1870–1879 Kelley-to-Aurin interval. **Their father could be Daniel Kelley**, but neither the 1870 marriage nor 1880 census directly records the girls' fathers. **Original birth records for each must be read**. In the 1880 original Jonas's household, both girls' father-birthplaces are entered *Sweden*, which conflicts with Daniel's **Michigan** birthplace reported in his 1870 marriage; this might reflect household stepfather reporting. Do not silently resolve.
4. **Amelia's proposed mother and stepfather**: witness **Betsey A. Cotton** in 1870 + original 1880 household-next-door **Samuel & Betsey A. Rackliff** with stepdaughter Lillie + original 1900 next-door **Samuel & Betsey A. Rackliff** + independent 1886 Montague family history about **Betsey Ann Brockway first married John West Cotton and remarried Samuel Rackleff** constitute strong converging evidence of the same maternal family. **The witness's mother relationship is an inference supported by historical family history, not an explicit relation field in this marriage form**. Marriages between distinct people using similar names cannot be absolutely excluded without fuller kinship original.
5. **Daniel Kelley / Harriet Hattie Cotton Kelley name collision remains important:** historical Montague 1886 names Amelia's younger sister **Hattie Cornelia Cotton married Kelley**. The 1870 original definitively names Amelia L. Cotton marrying **Daniel Kelley**. It is possible surname coincidence, later marriage to same Daniel, or error in derivative compiled families. **Do not assign Hattie's husband Daniel, or infer polygamy/two simultaneous marriages, without independent original**. If the 1875/1877 birth original records Daniel as father and Amelia mother, it would decisively strengthen child paternity and explain the subsequent Swedish-birthplace discrepancy.
6. **Jonas's fate is still unresolved.** Amelia married Jonas Feb 1879, bore Jessie Oct 1879 and Julius Nov 1881, then remarried Smith McConnell Oct 1888. None of these records proves when/how the Jonas marriage ended or his Swedish origins. Parallel possible independent 1853-born Massachusetts Charles Auren family is **probably another man**, not a settled tree change. First/last tax roll and naturalization/death/divorce evidence remain high-priority.
