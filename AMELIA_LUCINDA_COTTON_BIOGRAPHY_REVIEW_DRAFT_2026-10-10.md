## Biography — draft for person-by-person review

Amelia Lucinda Cotton was born in northern New York in 1853, one of the children of John West Cotton and Betsey Ann Brockway. Her parents had married in Pitcairn, New York, in November 1847. Over the following years their household grew, with children born into a region of small farms, river corridors and communities scattered across the northern part of the state.

While Amelia was still young, the Cottons made a major move west to Michigan. A family history published in 1886 identifies Amelia among five children who accompanied their father to Oceana County, a developing district inland from Lake Michigan. Her childhood and adolescence therefore stretched across two quite different landscapes: the settled towns and farms of northern New York and the wooded, recently organized townships of western Michigan.

The move brought the family into the changing economy of the lower Great Lakes. Timber and sawmills supported new villages while settlers cleared land for farms. Family life in this region involved a long transition from forest to fields, orchards and roads connecting communities that had only recently been incorporated. The surviving account places the Cotton family near Leavitt and Elbridge townships, not far from Crystal Valley.

Amelia was seventeen when her father died in Leavitt Township on 30 March 1870. His death came while the younger members of the household were still growing up, and the family continued in Oceana County. Two years later her mother married Samuel Rackleff. By then Amelia was entering adulthood in the Michigan community that would remain central to the next generation.

She became known by the married surname Aurin and was living in Crystal Valley when her extended family's history was recorded in the nineteenth century. Her sister Hattie Cornelia Cotton lived nearby under the surname Kelley. The sisters were part of the same community, though their households followed different courses in adulthood.

Amelia's daughter Jessie was recorded at birth in Crystal Township in October 1879 under a surname rendered *Auene* in the surviving index. At Jessie's marriage in 1899, her mother was named *Amelia Cotton*. Jessie's beginnings in Oceana County were part of the Michigan life Amelia had built there. In time, the younger generation would carry the family's story beyond the places of Amelia's own youth.

Across Amelia's lifetime, a family rooted in northern New York became part of a growing western Michigan community. Her generation carried that move; her daughter's would carry the family's story farther. Her family's movement from New York to Michigan formed an enduring connection between two regions of the Great Lakes.

## Historical setting — separate from biography

In mid-nineteenth-century northern New York, farming, timber, local industry and waterways connected the St. Lawrence region with expanding markets. The westward movement of families toward Michigan followed land availability and the rise of Great Lakes transport networks, though no specific reason for the Cottons' decision has yet been documented.

Oceana County formed part of a landscape long occupied and used by Odawa and other Anishinaabe communities. Euro-American settlement, treaty-era dispossession, sawmilling, roads and land clearing transformed the region in Amelia's youth. Inland townships such as Leavitt, Elbridge and Crystal were associated with both rural agriculture and the timber economy. These are the circumstances of her documented places, not claims about activities she personally undertook.
